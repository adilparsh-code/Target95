# ISC AI 883 — Board Practice Set 02

## Class XI

| Q | Type | Marks | Difficulty | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | MCQ | 1 | Easy | Which measure is least affected by an extreme value compared with the mean? | Median |
| 2 | MCQ | 1 | Easy | A histogram is primarily useful for showing: | Distribution of numerical data |
| 3 | Assertion–Reason | 2 | Medium | A: A line graph is useful for time-series trends. R: Consecutive observations can be connected to show change over time. | Both A and R are true, and R correctly explains A. |
| 4 | Short | 3 | Medium | Explain structured and unstructured data with one example each. | Structured data follows a defined tabular/schema-based format; unstructured data does not have a fixed tabular structure. |
| 5 | Numerical | 4 | Medium | For data 3, 5, 5, 7, 10, find mean, median and mode. | Mean = 6; Median = 5; Mode = 5. |
| 6 | Application | 4 | Medium | A dashboard shows daily website visits for 30 days. Which chart is appropriate and why? | Line chart, because it makes a chronological trend easy to observe. |
| 7 | HOTS | 5 | Hard | Explain why an outlier should not be removed automatically during preprocessing. | It may be a data-entry error, a rare but valid observation, or evidence of an important event. Context and investigation should guide treatment. |
| 8 | Case Based | 5 | Hard | A school uses an AI model to recommend academic support. The model uses attendance and marks but ignores contextual factors such as documented illness. Discuss two limitations and two safeguards. | Limitations: incomplete representation of student circumstances and possible unfair recommendations. Safeguards: human review, relevant contextual information, bias/fairness checks and transparent limitations. |

## Class XII

| Q | Type | Marks | Difficulty | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | MCQ | 1 | Easy | Which task normally predicts a continuous numerical value? | Regression |
| 2 | MCQ | 1 | Easy | Which learning approach groups observations without supplied target labels? | Unsupervised learning |
| 3 | Assertion–Reason | 2 | Medium | A: More complex models can overfit. R: A model can learn noise or training-specific patterns instead of general patterns. | Both A and R are true, and R correctly explains A. |
| 4 | Short | 3 | Medium | What is the purpose of splitting data into training and testing sets? | To train the model on one portion and evaluate generalisation on unseen data. |
| 5 | Numerical | 4 | Medium | A classifier has TP=50, TN=30, FP=10, FN=10. Find precision and recall. | Precision = 50/(50+10) = 83.33%; Recall = 50/(50+10) = 83.33%. |
| 6 | Application | 4 | Medium | Distinguish image classification from object detection. | Classification assigns labels to an image; object detection identifies objects and their locations within the image. |
| 7 | HOTS | 5 | Hard | Why can a model with lower overall accuracy sometimes be preferable in a high-risk application? | If it reduces the more harmful type of error or performs better on important minority cases, overall accuracy alone may not reflect practical usefulness. |
| 8 | Case Based | 5 | Hard | A computer-vision model performs well in the lab but poorly on images captured by different cameras. Give two possible causes and two improvement steps. | Causes: domain/data-distribution shift and differences in lighting, resolution or preprocessing. Improvements: add representative training data and standardise/validate preprocessing across environments. |

## Answering standard

For 3–5 mark answers, students should include the key concept plus a clear reason/example. For numerical questions, show formula, substitution and final result. For case studies, context-based equivalent answers are acceptable when logically justified.
