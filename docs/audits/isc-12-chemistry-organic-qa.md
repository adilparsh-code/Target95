### Syllabus text (Units 6–10), verbatim from the retrieved document

- **Paper I Theory 70 marks** = Physical Chemistry 25 (Solutions, Electrochemistry, Chemical Kinetics) + Inorganic Chemistry 14 (d- and f-Block, Coordination Compounds) + **Organic Chemistry 31** (Units 6–10). Paper II Practical 15, Project 10, Practical File 5.
- **Unit 6** — Haloalkanes: general formula, nomenclature, classification; nature of C–X bond; physical and chemical properties; **mechanism of substitution reactions**; **optical rotation**. Haloarenes: basic idea, nature of C–X bond, substitution reactions (**directive influence of halogen in monosubstituted compounds only**). Uses and environmental effects of dichloromethane, trichloromethane, tetrachloromethane, iodoform, freons, DDT. Named requirement list: preparation from alkane/halogen, alkene/HX, alcohols with PX₃/PCl₅/SOCl₂, **Finkelstein and Swarts**, **Hunsdiecker**; reactions with NaOH, water, NaI, ammonia, primary amine, secondary amine, KCN, AgCN, KNO₂, AgNO₂, silver salt of fatty acid, LiAlH₄; **Saytzeff/β-elimination**; **Wurtz and Grignard**; chloroform and iodoform; structure of freons; haloarene preparation by **Sandmeyer and Gattermann** and by electrophilic substitution; haloarene electrophilic substitution **with mechanism**; **nucleophilic substitution of haloarenes (replacement of Cl by –OH, –NH₂) with mechanism**; reduction to benzene; **Wurtz–Fittig**; **Fittig**; **addition of Mg**; structure of DDT.
- **Unit 7** — Alcohols: classification, general formula, structure, nomenclature; preparation (direct and indirect hydration, **hydroboration oxidation**, Grignard, hydrolysis of alkyl halides, reduction of carbonyls, **from primary amines**); **manufacture of methanol by Bosch process and ethanol by fermentation (outline only)**; properties — acidic nature, reaction with Na, **esterification with mechanism**, HX, PCl₃/PCl₅/SOCl₂, **acid chlorides and acid anhydrides**, oxidation, **dehydration with mechanism**; **uses with special reference to methanol and ethanol**; **conversion of one alcohol into another**; **distinction by Lucas' test**. Physical/chemical properties **of primary alcohols only**. Phenols: preparation from diazonium salt, **chlorobenzene (Dow)**, **benzene sulphonic acid**, **manufacture from cumene**; state and solubility; acidic character; reactions with NaOH, Na, **zinc**, acetyl chloride, acetic anhydride, PCl₅; bromination/nitration/sulphonation; **Kolbe**; **Reimer–Tiemann**; **FeCl₃ and azo dye tests**; uses. Ethers: structure, nomenclature, **Williamson**; state and miscibility; **chlorine**, **oxidation/peroxide formation**, **HI**, **PCl₅**; uses. **Aryl ethers: anisole by Williamson, electrophilic substitution (halogenation, nitration, Friedel–Crafts)**.
- **Unit 8** — Preparation from alcohol, alkenes (**ozonolysis**), alkynes (hydration), acid chlorides (**Rosenmund**, **dialkyl cadmium**), calcium salt, nitriles (**Stephen**, Grignard), esters; physical properties state and boiling point; nucleophilic addition **with mechanism** (ammonia and derivatives, HCN, NaHSO₃, Grignard); oxidation; iodoform; reduction (**Clemmensen**, **Wolff–Kishner**, **red P/HI**); base-catalysed **with mechanism** (aldol, cross-aldol, Cannizzaro); tests — **formaldehyde vs acetaldehyde**, aldehydes vs ketones; uses. Benzaldehyde: **lab preparation from toluene by chromyl chloride**; state and stability; oxidation/reduction; HCN and NaHSO₃; NH₃ derivatives; PCl₅; Cannizzaro; **benzoin**; **Perkin**; electrophilic substitution; distinction aromatic vs aliphatic aldehyde; uses. Carboxylic acids: **classification of mono and di carboxylic acids**; preparation from alcohols, aldehydes, nitriles, Grignard; physical properties state, boiling point, solubility; **effect of substituents on acidity — to be dealt with in detail**; reactions with active metals, alkalies, carbonates, bicarbonates; acid derivatives; **decarboxylation (chemical and Kolbe electrolytic)**; **HVZ**; **meta-directive effect** (nitration, sulphonation); tests for formic, acetic, benzoic; uses.
- **Unit 9** — Amines: nomenclature, classification, structure; preparation from alcohol, alkyl halide, cyanide, **amide (Hofmann degradation)**, nitro compound, **Gabriel phthalimide**; physical comparison (state, solubility, boiling point, vs alcohols); basic character vs ammonia/aniline and **effect of substituents on aniline**; **alkylation and acylation with mechanism**; **nitrous acid**; **carbylamine**; **Hinsberg**. Aniline: preparation by reduction of nitrobenzene; state, solubility, boiling point; HCl, H₂SO₄, acetylation, alkylation, **benzoylation**, carbylamine, **diazotisation**, electrophilic substitution; **tests for aniline**; **uses**. Cyanides and isocyanides: preparation. Diazonium: preparation; **Sandmeyer, Gattermann, Balz–Schiemann**; replacement by **–H, –OH, –NO₂**; **coupling with phenol and aniline**.
- **Unit 10** — Carbohydrates: definition; classification aldose/ketose; monosaccharides glucose and fructose, **D–L configuration**; oligosaccharides (**sucrose, lactose, maltose**); polysaccharides (starch, cellulose, glycogen); importance; reducing vs non-reducing; establishment of glucose and fructose structures (**HI, hydroxylamine, bromine water, acetic anhydride, nitric acid, phenylhydrazine**); **test for glucose and fructose, bromine water with equation**; **disaccharide structures showing glycosidic linkage**; polysaccharides. Proteins: amino acids, peptide bond, polypeptides; **classification on the basis of molecular shape**; primary–quaternary (**definitions only, details and diagrams not required**); denaturation; enzymes, hormones (elementary). Vitamins A, B, C, D, E, K — **classification and deficiency diseases, chemical names and structures not required**. Nucleic acids: **purine and pyrimidine as basic units**, DNA double helix, RNA, differences.

## Task 2 — Chemistry accuracy audit

Method: every learn block, table cell, figure description, model answer, explanation and `commonMistake` was read and checked against established organic chemistry. Numericals were recomputed from the stated molar masses.

### Defects found and repaired

| # | Chapter | Defect | Fix applied |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | Alcohols | Learn example claimed 2-methylpropan-2-ol can be made from **ethanal with two equivalents of methylmagnesium bromide**. Impossible: the first addition gives propan-2-ol, whose O–H is not a leaving group, so a second addition cannot occur. | Replaced with **ethyl ethanoate + 2 MeMgBr** (an ester genuinely accepts two additions), keeping the ketone route as the simpler one. |
| A2 | Alcohols | Preparation paragraph said alcohols are made "by **reducing** a primary amine with nitrous acid". Diazotisation of RNH₂ is not a reduction. | Reworded to "treating a primary amine with nitrous acid, which replaces the amino group by a hydroxyl group". |
| A3 | Aldehydes | `carbonyls-19` ("convert propan-1-ol into propan-2-one") had a model answer that produced **butan-2-one**, not propanone, and proposed propan-1-ol → 2-bromopropane, which is not what HBr gives from propan-1-ol. | Rewritten: propan-1-ol → propene (conc. H₂SO₄, 443 K) → propan-2-ol (steam/H₃PO₄) → propanone (acidified dichromate). |
| A4 | Haloalkanes | `haloalkanes-10` explanation said the Swarts reaction is driven by precipitation of "silver iodide or silver chloride"; silver iodide plays no part when AgF is the reagent. | Corrected to "silver chloride or silver bromide". |
| A5 | Haloalkanes | C–Cl bond length given as 176 pm in the CH₃X trend table. | Corrected to **178 pm** (139/178/193/214). |

### Verified correct (selected high-risk items)

- Bond-enthalpy trend 452/351/293/234 kJ mol⁻¹, boiling points 194/249/277/315 K, and the SN1/SN2 reactivity orders.
- Chlorobenzene: resonance + sp² carbon + unstable phenyl cation as the three reasons for low reactivity; ortho/para-directing **deactivating** classification; Dow conditions 623 K, 300 atm.
- Chloroform → phosgene, storage in dark, brim-filled bottles with about 1 % ethanol, which converts phosgene into diethyl carbonate (phosgene + 2 EtOH → diethyl carbonate + 2 HCl).
- Phenol pKa ≈ 10 vs ethanol ≈ 16 vs water 15.7; no CO₂ from NaHCO₃; FeCl₃ and azo-dye tests; cumene process giving phenol + acetone.
- Lucas observations (tertiary immediate, secondary ~5 min, primary on heating), dehydration order, oxidation products by class.
- Aldehyde/ketone reactivity order; Tollens/Fehling/Benedict/iodoform/Schiff behaviour table; **benzaldehyde reduces Tollens but not Fehling** (correct distinction); Clemmensen vs Wolff–Kishner reagent sets; pKa 0.65/4.20/4.76 for trichloroacetic/benzoic/acetic.
- Amine pKb table 3.27 / 3.38 / 4.22 / 4.75 / 9.38 / ≈13; aqueous order 2° > 1° > 3° > NH₃; gas-phase reversal; anilinium meta-directing rationale and the acetanilide protection strategy.
- All diazonium replacements (Cl, Br via Sandmeyer; Cu/HX Gattermann; BF₄⁻ Balz–Schiemann; KI; H₂O; H₃PO₂; NaNO₂/Cu) and both couplings.
- Glucose structure evidence chain (HI → n-hexane, NH₂OH → oxime, Br₂ water → gluconic acid, Ac₂O → penta-acetate, HNO₃ → saccharic acid, PhNHNH₂ → osazone); fructose enediol tautomerisation explaining Tollens-positive/bromine-negative; sucrose non-reducing because both anomeric carbons are committed; α-1,4 vs β-1,4 for starch vs cellulose.
- Numericals recomputed: 75.2 g phenol from 112.5 g chlorobenzene at 80 %; 11.1 g ethanoic acid from 10.0 g ethanol at 85 %; 5.4 g benzyl alcohol + 7.2 g sodium benzoate from 10.6 g benzaldehyde (mass balance 12.6 g checks against 10.6 g + 0.05 mol NaOH).

**Accuracy verdict by chapter:** Haloalkanes — 2 minor errors (repaired). Alcohols — 2 errors (repaired). Aldehydes — 1 substantive error (repaired). Nitrogen — **no errors found**. Biomolecules — **no errors found**.

## Task 4 — UI verification: **BLOCKED**

**The five chapters cannot be opened in the ChapterReader at all.**

`src/lib/boardprepCurriculum.mjs` → `getChapter()` resolves a chapter **only** from `getPublishedChapters()`, which filters `chapter.status === 'published'`. Every chapter in this milestone is `status: "planned"`. Consequently `/learn/isc/class-12/chemistry/<slug>` returns `notFound()` for all five, and there is no route, preview or dev URL that renders them.

Making them inspectable would require changing registry status or the read model — both explicitly prohibited by this task. **No visual, formatting, navigation or responsive inspection was performed, and none is claimed.**

**Unblocking options:** (a) add a development-only draft preview route, (b) approve a temporary status flip on a scratch branch purely for visual QA, or (c) accept that UI verification happens after academic approval, immediately before publication.
